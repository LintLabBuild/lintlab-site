Every security company says it can protect you. Very few can show you something they
built, broke and fixed themselves. That is the gap Lint is built around.

## Proof of work, not promises

We are a young lab. We don't have a long list of clients to put on a logo wall, and we
won't invent one. So we do the next best thing, which we think is actually the better
thing: we build real products and attack them ourselves.

- Our [contest platform](/work/lint-contest-platform/) runs untrusted code from
  strangers. If its sandbox fails, we're the ones who get hurt.
- [SecureCode](/work/securecode/) only works if we understand vulnerabilities well
  enough to explain them to students.
- Our [router project](/work/router-reverse-engineering/) starts with a board that has
  no datasheet and ends at the registers.

When a company asks "can you secure our system?", we can point at systems we have
designed for load and attack, and show how we did it.

## Products and services feed each other

Building products teaches us where systems really break. Client work, when it comes,
shows us problems we would never have found alone. Each makes the other better, which
is why we don't plan to choose between them.

## Where students come in

While we build, we bring in students who have the spark. They work on the same
industry-style projects, get an experience certificate from a registered company, and
join our network. When paid work arrives, they are the first people we call. You can
read how that works on the [Lint Lab page](/lab/).

## What we won't do

We won't test anything without written permission. We won't claim experience we
don't have. And we'll keep telling you which of our projects is live, which is a
prototype and which is research.

If you want a security review, a secure exam environment, or someone to look inside a
device, [talk to us](/contact/).
